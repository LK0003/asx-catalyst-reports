Section 8 — Best 3 to Research Further

1. BMN (Bannerman Energy)

Why it made the list: The only major fresh catalyst that remains genuinely unpriced — the market hasn't reacted yet because the stock is still halted.

What the market may be reacting to (once reopened): Removal of financing uncertainty for Etango and confirmation of a well-capitalised JV partner (CNOL).

What would confirm the thesis: A reopen price at/above the $4.00 placement price, and/or analyst commentary treating the CNOL completion as genuine de-risking.

What would invalidate it: A reopen well below $4.00, or any sign the CNOL conditions aren't as fully satisfied as described.

Biggest risk: Unknown reopen price — genuine two-sided risk given no trading since the announcement.

What to monitor: Halt-lift timing (by Fri 11 Sept latest), reopen price/volume, formal CNOL completion within September.

2. PXX (PolarX)

Why it made the list: Rare, genuinely high-grade (near-10% Cu) drill intercepts with a reported downhole mineralisation extension — a real geological surprise.

What the market may be reacting to: The prospect of a future resource upgrade at Caribou Dome.

What would confirm the thesis: A formal resource upgrade, continued high-grade results from the remaining ~1,700m of drilling, or increased broker coverage.

What would invalidate it: Historical pulp re-assays failing to corroborate the twin-hole grades, or subsequent drilling returning materially lower grades.

Biggest risk: Single-asset micro-cap with thin liquidity — drill-stage news alone doesn't guarantee an economic outcome.

What to monitor: Remaining drilling results this season; any formal resource-update timeline.

3. ASB (Austal)

Why it made the list: A live, unresolved bidding-war dynamic for a major asset, with real optionality for a higher eventual outcome — even though yesterday's move is already priced in.

What the market may be reacting to: Speculation that competitive tension between Wildcat and Hanwha could push the eventual sale price toward or above the top of the disclosed ranges.

What would confirm the thesis: A formal, binding proposal from either party, or evidence of an actual raised offer.

What would invalidate it: Wildcat's four-week due diligence concluding without a binding offer, or Hanwha (an existing ~19.9% shareholder) blocking a sale to a rival.

Biggest risk: Both proposals remain non-binding — no certainty a transaction completes at all, and the stock has already re-rated on speculation alone.

What to monitor: Any Hanwha statement, a fresh ASX trading halt, news from the four-week Wildcat due-diligence window.
