1. EUR / CRML — European Lithium / Critical Metals Corp Score 38/100 Price-sensitive: Y Acquisition / Scheme of arrangement 🟡

Supreme Court of WA approves scheme meetings; meeting dates and independent expert opinion now confirmed.

Announcement time

Court hearing held ~9:15am AWST (~11:15am AEST) today. This is the item flagged in the 12:38pm sweep as "most likely to have fresh information" — it now does.

Why it matters

The Court has ordered EUR to hold a Share Scheme Meeting and Option Scheme Meeting, clearing the way for Critical Metals Corp's all-scrip acquisition of 100% of European Lithium to proceed to a vote. Meeting dates are fixed for Thursday, 22 October 2026 (General Meeting 10:00am AWST, Share Scheme Meeting 10:30am AWST, Option Scheme Meeting 11:00am AWST or on conclusion of the Share Scheme Meeting), West Leederville, WA. More interesting: the independent expert concluded the Share Scheme is "not fair but reasonable" to EUR shareholders (Option Scheme assessed as fair and reasonable), both conditional on no superior proposal emerging — an unusual split verdict.

What changed / financial impact

Procedural gate cleared as expected; new information is the fixed 22 October date and the expert's split fairness conclusion. No change to deal terms, but the "not fair" component could feed proxy-adviser/shareholder scrutiny into the vote.

Expectation/surprise · Market context

Base case (court approval) was met; the expert's language is a modest negative surprise on valuation optics, not on completion probability. Lithium sector remains volatile; EUR shares reportedly up ~137% into this court date — a lot of good news may already be priced in.

Key risks
  • "Not fair but reasonable" opinions occasionally draw proxy-adviser or activist shareholder pushback ahead of a scheme vote.
  • Six-week gap to the meeting leaves the all-scrip deal exposed to further lithium-price moves.
What to watch

EUR's scheme booklet dispatch and any proxy-adviser (ISS/CGI Glass Lewis) commentary ahead of 22 October.

4. EQR — EQ Resources Score 44/100 Price-sensitive: Y Partnership / Resource development (update) 🟢

Fuller Springer Tungsten JV terms now public (still nominally halted per this morning's notice).

Announcement time

Terms reported via secondary sources through the day; ASX halt notice states resumption by 16 September 2026 or on further announcement — not verified whether EQR has resumed trading as of this scan.

Why it matters

Fuller terms: The Elmet Group holds 70% and is operator, Blue Moon Metals holds 20% (contributing the existing plant), EQ Resources holds 10%. Critically, EQR's own capital exposure is capped at US$3.125m even if total restart costs run to US$100m — a much smaller, more asymmetric commitment than the headline "US$150–175m investment" figure in this morning's report implied for EQR specifically (that figure was Elmet's total investment, not EQR's). In exchange EQR reportedly secures a board seat, a five-year right to 1,000 tpa of APT processing capacity, an eight-year spot-priced offtake for 4,000 tonnes of contained WO3, and a probable ore-sorting technology licence. The US Department of War is reported to be helping fund Elmet's US$75m restart bill.

What changed / financial impact

Clarification of EQR's actual (capped) economic exposure and specific offtake/processing rights — meaningfully de-risks the "how much is EQ on the hook for" question flagged as unresolved this morning.

Key risks
  • Still described as a "binding framework agreement," not a fully unconditional transaction — TSXV consent and other conditions likely still apply.
  • EQR remains apparently halted; rely on ASX-released terms once confirmed.
What to watch

Confirmation the halt has lifted and that ASX-released terms match the figures reported above.
