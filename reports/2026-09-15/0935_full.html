1. ASB — Austal Limited Score 58/100 Price-sensitive: Y Trading halt / Acquisition / Takeover speculation 🟡

Shares remain suspended amid live US$1.25–1.9bn bidding war for Austal USA.

Announcement time

Trading paused by ASX Supervision 9 September 2026; no confirmed reinstatement found as of this scan. Not verified whether the suspension is still active at this exact moment.

Why it matters

Austal's US shipbuilding arm (Austal USA) is subject to a competing-bidder auction: South Korea's Hanwha (already owns 19.9% of Austal) has a non-binding proposal at US$1.05–1.2bn and is in due diligence; Florida-based Wildcat Infrastructure has tabled a higher indication of US$1.25–1.35bn (one source cited $1.9bn — not independently confirmed) with its own four-week due-diligence window from ~9 September. Austal USA is a large share of group earnings, so the eventual sale price/structure is highly material.

What changed / financial impact

A second, higher bidder emerged for a subsidiary already under a formal sale process; ASX-Supervision-imposed pause since 9 September, not yet confirmed reinstated. Financial impact is very high in magnitude but unquantifiable pre-open given no binding agreement exists with either party.

Expectation/surprise · Market context · Technical

Not verified — no consensus sale-price estimate exists. Sits inside a hot US/Australian defence-industrial shipbuilding theme (AUKUS, US Navy capacity). Technical setup not verified while halted; last quoted near multi-week highs (~A$4.60–4.74) before suspension.

Key risks
  • Both bids are non-binding indications only; either could be withdrawn or fail due diligence/regulatory hurdles (CFIUS, Special Security Agreement).
  • A suspension this long, imposed by ASX Supervision rather than requested by the company, is unusual and may signal an unresolved disclosure issue beyond the bidding itself — not verified, flagged as a risk to watch.
  • Resolution could move the stock sharply in either direction once reinstated.
What to watch

Whether ASB remains suspended at open; any overnight announcement naming a board recommendation, extended due diligence, or a bidder withdrawal.

Sources: The Nightly · Finance News Network · Motley Fool · Kalkine

Section 8 — Best 3 to Research Further

1. ASB — Austal Limited

Why it made the list: The single largest live financial event in this scan — a competitive, above-market bidding process for a subsidiary worth a large fraction of Austal's own market capitalisation, currently frozen in an unusually long ASX-Supervision suspension.

What the market may be reacting to (once reinstated): Whichever bidder's terms, or lack of resolution, is confirmed; also possible scrutiny of why the suspension has run longer than a standard trading halt.

What would confirm/invalidate the thesis: A confirmed board recommendation, a binding agreement with either Hanwha or Wildcat, or conversely a suspension extension/withdrawal of a bidder.

Biggest risk: Regulatory conditions (CFIUS, Special Security Agreement) could stall or kill either bid regardless of price.

What to monitor: ASX announcements platform directly for ASB; reinstatement timing.

2. EQR — EQ Resources

Why it made the list: A concrete, dollar-figured US critical-minerals JV with a named industrial partner (Elmet) and a US$150–175m headline investment number, layered on an existing high-profile shareholder (Andrew Forrest) narrative.

What the market may be reacting to: Confirmation (or not) of EQ's exact economic interest and whether a capital raise accompanies the announcement.

What would confirm/invalidate the thesis: The actual ASX-released announcement text, once the halt lifts, reconciled against the newswire version already public.

Biggest risk: Deal remains conditional on due diligence, definitive documentation and TSXV consent; a capital raise, if attached, could dilute the upside.

What to monitor: EQR's ASX announcements page for the halt-lifting release.

3. Gold sector (NST / GMD / EVN / RMS) into the FOMC decision

Why it made the list: A clear, dated (15–16 September) macro binary directly affecting a sector that has already been derating from 2026 highs — this is a real, near-term catalyst rather than background noise.

What the market may be reacting to: Repricing of rate-hike odds ahead of Wednesday's (US time) decision; gold miners are geared plays on bullion and real yields.

What would confirm/invalidate the thesis: The actual FOMC statement/dot plot on 16 September; a hold would likely relieve pressure on gold equities, a hike would likely extend the unwind.

Biggest risk: This is a market-wide macro event, not a stock-specific catalyst — position sizing and correlation across the sector should be considered, not single-name selection.

What to monitor: Gold spot price and the ASX Gold Sub-Index into and after the Fed decision.
