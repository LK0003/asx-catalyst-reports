- **Why it made the list:** An 8-month formal strategic review with chair commentary already hinting at a "proposed combination" is exactly the kind of situation where a single morning announcement can resolve a long-standing valuation overhang either way.
- **What the market may be reacting to:** Potential confirmation (or denial) of a sale/combination transaction after months of "review remains active" language.
- **What would confirm/invalidate the thesis:** Confirm = named counterparty, binding terms, implied valuation/premium. Invalidate = generic "review continues, no certainty of transaction" language repeated from prior updates.
- **Biggest risk:** Reading too much into a routine quarterly update bundled with the review language — OFX has repeated "ongoing, no certainty" messaging for 8+ months.
- **What to monitor:** Opening price/volume reaction; any same-day broker notes naming a counterparty.

### 2. BSL (BlueScope Steel)
- **Why it made the list:** Large-cap, confirmed guidance-upgrade headline with a well-documented prior guidance baseline ($860–960m 1H FY27 underlying EBIT) to measure the surprise against.
- **What the market may be reacting to:** Evidence that US/Asian steel spreads are running ahead of BlueScope's own (already-raised) expectations from August.
- **What would confirm/invalidate the thesis:** Confirm = new EBIT range materially above $960m with a stated driver (spread widening, volume). Invalidate = a token upward revision inside/near the existing range, which the market could read as "not enough" given BSL actually fell ~9.7% on its last (positive) guidance release.
- **Biggest risk:** BSL's own history shows good news can still produce a negative share-price reaction if it's already priced in — don't assume 🟢 direction just because the headline says "upgrade."
- **What to monitor:** The exact new EBIT figure vs. $860–960m; opening price reaction; read-through to SGM and building-materials names.

### 3. DTR (Dateline Resources)
- **Why it made the list:** A live US federal court event firing today, with both the company and the US Government (DOJ/Dept of War) aligned in seeking a stay — an unusual degree of federal-government intervention in support of a mining operator, and exactly the kind of offshore, non-ASX-native catalyst this report is designed to catch.
- **What the market may be reacting to:** DTR has reportedly already traded up on news of DOJ/Dept of War support for the stay motion — the market may be pricing in a reasonable probability of a favourable ruling.
- **What would confirm/invalidate the thesis:** Confirm = court grants the stay, operations resume. Invalidate = stay denied, injunction continues, next step shifts to the Ninth Circuit appeal with no near-term resolution.
- **Biggest risk:** Court rulings are frequently taken under advisement rather than decided from the bench same-day — there may be no news at all today despite the hearing occurring.
- **What to monitor:** US newswire sources (Access Newswire, webdisclosure) rather than ASX announcements for the fastest read on any ruling; any DTR trading-halt request.
