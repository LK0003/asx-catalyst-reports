1. 🔴 MXT / MOT / MRE — Metrics Credit Partners listed funds

Type: Trading Halt / Regulatory-Audit Price-sensitive: Y Score: 66/100 Time: 28 Sep 2026 (unresolved)

Announcement: Three ASX-listed funds managed by Metrics Credit Partners (~A$40bn manager; responsible entity Perpetual) voluntarily suspended trading after auditor KPMG made different judgement calls on inputs/probability weightings versus the funds' unaudited 31 August preliminary financial reports, producing materially different audited net tangible asset (NTA) values. KPMG's finalised audited financial statements are due 30 September 2026 (tomorrow).

Why it matters: A rare event — an auditor materially disagreeing with a listed fund manager's own preliminary NTA marks, right before finalisation. It directly cuts unit backing (not just sentiment) across three retail-heavy income/credit trusts worth roughly A$3bn combined, and raises valuation-governance questions across Metrics' broader private-credit platform.

What changed: Confirmed audited NTA reductions of 12.16% (MRE), 10.08% (MOT) and 1.99% (MXT) versus the 31 August preliminary figures.

Financial impact: Direct, quantified capital-value hit to unit NTA — not merely reputational. MRE's ~12% cut is the standout; MXT's ~2% cut is modest given its more senior/diversified loan book.

Expectation/surprise: Genuine negative surprise — the divergence emerged only through the audit process. Not verified against a consensus estimate (income trusts aren't typically covered for EPS consensus).

Market/sector context: Comes amid live global scrutiny of private-credit valuation methodology; a restatement at Metrics' scale may prompt read-through to peer listed private-credit vehicles.

Technical context: Not verified — units are suspended, no live price action.

Key risks:

  • Suspension could extend past the 30 September deadline.
  • Read-through risk to peer Metrics-managed or other private-credit listed vehicles.
  • Reputational/governance risk for Metrics and Perpetual.

What to watch: Confirmation the 30 September audited FY26 accounts are lodged; resumption (or further extension) of trading; commentary on remediation of valuation processes; post-writedown distribution guidance.

Sources: Money Management · Capital Brief · Business News Australia · Reuters via Investing.com

2. 🟡 NST — Northern Star Resources

Type: Takeover Price-sensitive: Y Score: 68/100 Time: 27 Sep 2026 (ongoing)

Announcement: Northern Star's board rejected an unsolicited, non-binding, conditional proposal from Gold Fields valuing Northern Star at an implied ~A$38.7bn (~US$27.1bn), calling it opportunistic. Gold Fields said it intends to press on; activist investor Elliott Management has separately pushed Northern Star toward a strategic review/sale process since June.

Why it matters: Potentially the largest M&A situation in the ASX gold sector this cycle. A combined entity would be the world's second-largest gold producer (~4.1Moz/year), 80% Australia/North America/Chile production, with Northern Star holders retaining 33% of the enlarged group if a deal proceeds.

What changed: Formal board rejection (based on an 11 September share-price reference), with Gold Fields publicly signalling it isn't dropping the pursuit.

Financial impact: A$38.7bn implied valuation is a material premium reference point; the next move drives near-term price action independent of gold-price moves.

Expectation/surprise: The rejection itself is not a surprise; the real catalyst still to come is whether Gold Fields returns with a bump, goes hostile, or walks away.

Market/sector context: Comes the same day gold fell ~3–4% overnight — separate the M&A-premium effect from the commodity-price effect when reading NST's share price (see item 5).

Technical context: Not verified — no primary chart data accessed this run.

Key risks:

  • Gold Fields could walk away entirely.
  • FIRB/regulatory scrutiny given ~80% Australian production exposure.
  • Integration risk if a deal proceeds; gold-price downside independent of the M&A story.

What to watch: Any formal indicative or binding proposal from Gold Fields; Elliott Management's positioning; NST price relative to non-takeover-target gold peers.

Sources: US News/Reuters · Bloomberg · CNBC · Miningmx

Expectation/surprise: Broadly in line with expectations of an extended process; no major surprise this run.

Market/sector context: Financial-services/wealth-platform M&A has been an active ASX theme this year.
