### 1. MXT / MOT / MRE — Metrics Credit Partners listed funds (Metrics Master Income Trust, Metrics Income Opportunities Trust, Metrics Real Estate Multi-Strategy Fund)
- **Announcement time:** Voluntary suspension requested 28 September 2026 (yesterday), status unresolved into this morning.
- **Announcement:** Three ASX-listed funds managed by Metrics Credit Partners (~A$40bn manager; responsible entity Perpetual) voluntarily suspended trading after auditor KPMG made different judgement calls on inputs/probability weightings versus the funds' unaudited 31 August preliminary financial reports, producing materially different audited net tangible asset (NTA) values. KPMG's finalised audited financial statements are due 30 September 2026 (tomorrow).
- **Catalyst type:** Trading Halt / Regulatory-Audit — Price-sensitive: **Y**
- **Catalyst Score: 66/100** (Materiality 20/25, Surprise 15/20, Financial impact 15/20, Sector/macro support 4/10, Liquidity/tradability 4/10 — currently untradeable, Technical 3/10 not verified, Freshness 5/5)
- **Direction:** 🔴
- **Why it matters:** This is a rare event — an auditor materially disagreeing with a listed fund manager's own preliminary NTA marks, right before finalisation. It directly cuts unit backing (not just sentiment) across three retail-heavy income/credit trusts with a combined market value of roughly A$3bn, and raises questions about valuation governance across Metrics' broader private-credit platform.
- **What changed:** Confirmed audited NTA reductions of **12.16%** (Metrics Real Estate Multi-Strategy Fund), **10.08%** (Metrics Income Opportunities Trust) and **1.99%** (Metrics Master Income Trust) versus the preliminary unaudited figures released 31 August.
- **Financial impact:** Direct, quantified capital-value hit to unit NTA — not merely reputational. MRE's ~12% cut is the standout; MXT's ~2% cut is comparatively modest given its more senior/diversified loan book.
- **Expectation/surprise:** Genuine negative surprise — preliminary results were released 31 August without flagging this scale of discrepancy; the divergence emerged only through KPMG's audit process. **Not verified** against a consensus estimate (these are income trusts, not typically covered for EPS consensus).
- **Market/sector context:** Comes at a sensitive time for the broader private-credit/direct-lending sector, where valuation-methodology scrutiny has been a live global theme; a high-profile NTA restatement at a manager of Metrics' scale may prompt read-through scrutiny of other listed private-credit vehicles.
- **Technical context:** Not verified — units are suspended, no live price action to assess.
- **Key risks:**
  - Suspension could extend past the 30 September deadline if KPMG/Metrics need more time to finalise.
  - Read-through risk to other Metrics-managed or peer private-credit listed vehicles.
  - Reputational/governance risk for Metrics as manager and Perpetual as responsible entity.
- **What to watch:** Confirmation that the 30 September audited FY26 accounts are actually lodged; the resumption (or further extension) of trading; any commentary from Metrics/Perpetual on remediation of valuation processes; distribution guidance post-writedown.
- **Sources:** [Money Management](https://www.moneymanagement.com.au/three-metrics-funds-suspended-after-audited-results-uncover-material-differences), [Capital Brief](https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/metrics-listed-funds-suspended-from-quotation-ahead-of-audited-financials-93df3dfb-5d1e-4351-b7a4-857fd19b5fec/), [Business News Australia](https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/three-metrics-funds-halt-asx-trading-after-kpmg-uncovers-discrepancies-in-net-asset-backing.html), [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/major-australian-private-lender-suspends-three-asx-funds-cuts-asset-values-after-audit-4919329)

### 2. NST — Northern Star Resources
- **Announcement time:** Board rejection reported 27 September 2026; ongoing into this week.
- **Announcement:** Northern Star's board rejected an unsolicited, non-binding, conditional proposal from South Africa's Gold Fields valuing Northern Star at an implied ~A$38.7bn (~US$27.1bn), calling it opportunistic. Gold Fields has said it intends to press on; activist investor Elliott Management has separately been pushing Northern Star toward a strategic review/sale process since June.
- **Catalyst type:** Takeover — Price-sensitive: **Y**
- **Catalyst Score: 68/100** (Materiality 22/25, Surprise 8/20 — a bid/rejection cycle was broadly anticipated given the Elliott campaign, Financial impact 16/20, Sector/macro 6/10, Liquidity/tradability 9/10, Technical 3/10 not verified, Freshness 4/5)
- **Direction:** 🟡 (genuinely two-sided: rejection could mean a walk-away, or the opening move in a higher-bid contest)
- **Why it matters:** This is potentially the largest M&A situation in the ASX gold sector this cycle. A combined entity would be the world's second-largest gold producer (~4.1Moz/year) with 80% of production from Australia/North America/Chile, and Northern Star holders would retain 33% of the enlarged group if a deal proceeds — meaningful upside optionality even at a "rejected" valuation.
- **What changed:** Formal board rejection of Gold Fields' proposal (based on an 11 September share-price reference), with Gold Fields publicly signalling it isn't dropping the pursuit.
- **Financial impact:** A$38.7bn implied valuation is a material premium reference point; whether Gold Fields returns with a bump, and how, will drive near-term price action independent of gold-price moves.
- **Expectation/surprise:** Rejection itself is not a surprise (boards almost never accept a first opportunistic approach), but the next move (a formal on-market bid, a sweetened proposal, or Gold Fields walking away) is the actual catalyst still to come.
- **Market/sector context:** Comes on the same day gold prices fell ~3–4% overnight — a genuine cross-current: gold-price weakness is a headwind for NST's standalone earnings even as takeover speculation supports the stock. Do not read a rising NST share price purely as bullish sentiment without separating the M&A-premium effect from the commodity-price effect.
- **Technical context:** Not verified — no primary chart data accessed this run.
- **Key risks:** Gold Fields could walk away entirely; regulatory/foreign-investment (FIRB) scrutiny of a scheme with 80% Australian production exposure; integration risk if a deal proceeds; gold-price downside independent of the M&A story.
- **What to watch:** Any formal indicative or binding proposal from Gold Fields; Elliott Management's public positioning; NST share-price reaction relative to peers (to isolate M&A premium from gold-price beta).
- **Sources:** [US News/Reuters](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-09-27/australias-northern-star-rejects-27-billion-takeover-approach-from-gold-fields), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-27/northern-star-rejects-opportunistic-proposal-from-gold-fields), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/28/northern-star-shares-gold-fields-takeover-bid.html), [Miningmx](https://www.miningmx.com/top-story/67541-gold-fields-goes-hostile-in-27bn-northern-star-takeover/)
