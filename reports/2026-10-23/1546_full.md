- **Why it matters:** This is the single biggest market-moving event of the day so far. The market's reaction (shares down ~30% intraday to ~A$1.45–1.45, now down >60% over the past year per Rask Media) shows credit-quality and fraud-control concerns are outweighing the headline growth numbers.

FY27 guidance (group cash EBTDA A$340m, US TTV growth >30% USD, 20–22% operating margin) was explicitly reaffirmed.

The reaffirmed guidance and "beat on most key metrics" framing (per RBC Capital Markets' Julian Mulcahy, who kept an Outperform rating and A$2.07 price target) argue the sell-off is overdone on a pure-numbers basis; conversely, a second consecutive credit-quality wobble (Mulcahy flagged this episode's resemblance to the FY26 Q2 loss-rate shock) raises a real question about whether ZIP's rapid US TTV growth is outrunning its fraud/credit controls.
- **Expectation/surprise:** Very high. Guidance had just been set in August and the market had no warning of a fraud event; the scale of the single-day price reaction (~30%) vastly exceeds what the reported metrics alone would suggest, indicating the market is pricing in a genuine trust/quality-of-earnings concern rather than just this quarter's numbers.
- **Market/sector context:** BNPL/consumer-fintech sector read-through is negative (NEUTRAL→CONTRADICTED shift versus this morning's "NEUTRAL, pending ZIP's print" call) — watch for sympathy weakness in other BNPL/fintech names (e.g. Sezzle-exposed, Block/Afterpay-adjacent names) into the close.

- **Key risks:**
  - Second fraud/credit-quality scare within roughly a year raises a structural question about risk controls scaling with US growth, not just a one-off.
  - Active customer and merchant counts fell q/q (merchants down after exiting New Zealand) — a growth-quality flag independent of the fraud event.
