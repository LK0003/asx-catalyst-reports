Section 8 — Best 3 to Research Further

1. INA (Ingenia Communities Group)

Why it made the list: The single most material, genuinely new-today catalyst — a $1.9bn takeover proposal rejected by the board, with the market reportedly reacting positively rather than punishing the rejection.

What the market may be reacting to: A view that the rejection validates a higher standalone value and/or raises the odds of an improved offer, rather than disappointment that a deal didn't proceed.

What would confirm/invalidate: A revised, higher proposal from Warburg Pincus (confirms) versus silence/walk-away and the stock drifting back toward pre-news levels (invalidates the "higher bid coming" read, though the standalone growth thesis, including the Peet acquisition, would remain).

Biggest risk: Treating a rejected non-binding indicative proposal as if a deal is assured.

What to monitor: Any follow-up ASX notice from Ingenia or Warburg Pincus; INA's close today vs $4.75; progress of the Peet scheme (meeting expected ~December 2026).

2. BUB (Bubs Australia)

Why it made the list: A binary regulatory risk (US FDA market access) has resolved positively, removing a standing overhang on Bubs' largest revenue market, with a large reported price reaction.

What the market may be reacting to: Confirmation of continued, permanent access to the US market for three specific products — a genuine addressable-market/revenue-security event, not just positive-sounding language.

What would confirm/invalidate: Follow-up commentary on production capacity and actual US sales execution would confirm a durable re-rating; a subsequent capital raise or a scope-limited reading of the authorisation would partially invalidate the enthusiasm.

Biggest risk: The reported +29–40% range is unverified against a live quote.

What to monitor: BUB's actual closing price/volume; any ASX announcement clarifying the FDA authorisation's exact scope.

3. WDS (Woodside Energy)

Why it made the list: A live example of a flagged anomaly (falling stock despite rising oil) apparently self-correcting within the same session — useful both as a signal and as a case study in why single-snapshot, WebSearch-derived price data shouldn't be trusted without a live-quote check.

What the market may be reacting to: The weekend's Hormuz escalation and OPEC+'s decision to hold October output steady, both supply-risk-positive for producers, after an initial (likely stale-data-driven) morning read suggesting the opposite.

What would confirm/invalidate: WDS closing meaningfully higher, in line with oil, confirms the sector-rally read; a fade back to flat/negative by the close would suggest the midday "energy is the star sector" framing was overstated or short-lived.

Biggest risk: A ceasefire or de-escalation headline before the close reversing both oil and the energy trade quickly.

What to monitor: WDS/STO closing prints; Brent/WTI into tonight's US session; any Hormuz ceasefire or further-escalation headlines.
