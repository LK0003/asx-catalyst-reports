Score 72/100 🔴 Negative (this round) Takeover/M&A Price-sensitive: Yes

Announcement time: Reported this morning (21 Sept); board statement said engagement with EQT "has now concluded."

Announcement: Perpetual's board rejected EQT's (Windflower Pte) revised, self-described "best and final" A$22.50/share proposal — which added a mechanism allowing Perpetual to pay an interim dividend of up to 60cps without adjusting scheme consideration — citing an "unacceptable degree of transaction execution risk." This is a material update, not a repeat, on the AM report's #1 item. This closes out (for now) the largest live M&A situation on the ASX.

What changed

From "rejected, but data room opened" (AM framing) to "rejected, and engagement formally ended" (confirmed by early afternoon), plus a now-observable, large negative price reaction that was "Not verified" as recently as the 10:16 sweep.

Financial impact

PPT fell from Friday's ~A$19.60 close to an intraday low of ~A$16.50 (as much as -15.8%), with at least one afternoon aggregator describing the fall as "13pc." Exact closing-price move not verified — figures from different snapshots/times conflict by a few percentage points, consistent with an intraday recovery from the low.

Expectation / surprise

A genuine negative surprise relative to the AM report's more constructive read; less of a surprise relative to the pattern of this saga (EQT has walked and returned multiple times since June/July).

Market/sector context

Financials sector reported under renewed selling pressure this session; this is a stock-specific, not sector-driven, move.

Technical context

A single-session double-digit-percentage decline; PPT is now trading well below both the $22.50 proposal price and its own pre-weekend levels. Not independently chart-verified.

Key risks

  • Conflicting percentage-fall figures (13% vs ~15.8%) across sources mean the exact scale of today's move is not pinned down.

2.RSG — Resolute Mining
Score 61/100 🔴 Negative Guidance down / Production Price-sensitive: Yes

Announcement time: Reported today (21 Sept); not present in any of today's three earlier reports — genuinely new to this report series.

Announcement: Resolute Mining cut full-year Syama (Mali) gold production guidance to 150,000–160,000oz at AISC A$2,300–2,400/oz, pulling Group guidance down to 205,000–225,000oz at A$2,250–2,350/oz. Syama produced only 74koz in H1 2026 (Q2: 30koz; July–August combined just 15.5koz) due to in-country logistics/security disruptions delaying equipment needed for the A21 open pit and an explosives supply squeeze underground. Q4 recovery to 45–50koz is contingent on a new mining contractor mobilising in Sept–Oct and an on-site emulsion plant commissioning by November.

Why it matters

This is the clearest example this run of the "rising headline vs. negative substance" test working in reverse — gold prices are near multi-month highs, yet RSG is being punished because company-specific execution problems (not the gold price) are compressing near-term production and margin. It is also a useful counter-read to the AM report's uniformly positive framing of the gold-miner sector basket.

What changed

Formal downward revision to previously-guided FY26 Syama and Group production; Q4 recovery is explicitly conditional on two operational fixes not yet delivered.

Financial impact

Guidance cut directly reduces FY26 gold-ounce output and raises unit costs (AISC guided up to $2,300–2,400/oz at Syama) — a clear negative earnings revision, though exact dollar EBITDA/consensus impact not verified.

Expectation / surprise

A negative surprise — the cut follows a run of already-weak quarterly production, but the magnitude (guidance nearly halved at Syama versus the original 195–210koz range cited by wire reports) is more severe than a simple "in-line, lower end" outcome.

Market/sector context

Runs counter to the broadly positive gold-price/gold-miner narrative in today's session and in the AM report — a reminder that a strong commodity price does not protect a producer from execution risk.

Technical context

Shares reportedly touched an intraday 2026 low near A$1.02 before recovering to around A$1.14–1.24 (sources vary), down roughly 5–8% on the day and ~12% on the week. Not independently chart-verified.

Key risks
  • Mali country risk (security, logistics, regulatory) is structural, not a one-off — repeat disruption risk into FY27.
  • Q4 recovery depends on two discrete operational deliverables not yet delivered.
  • Conflicting source figures on the exact share-price decline and recovery level.
What to watch into/after the close

Confirmed closing price; any broker note re-rating FY27 production/cost assumptions; progress updates on contractor mobilisation and the emulsion plant.

4.EBR — EBR Systems
Score 44/100 🟢 Positive Regulatory / Reimbursement Price-sensitive: Yes

Announcement time: Today (21 Sept), per Kalkine.

Announcement: The US Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) finalised increased Medicare inpatient reimbursement rates for EBR's WiSE cardiac-pacing system under the FY2027 Inpatient Prospective Payment System Final Rule, effective 1 October 2026 — reassigning eligible WiSE procedures to higher-value MS-DRG categories, lifting base payment rates by ~58% (MS-DRG 242→228) and ~93% (MS-DRG 244→229).

Why it matters

A confirmed, quantified (58–93%) increase in US hospital payment rates directly reduces a key adoption barrier (hospital margin on the procedure) for a single-product medtech name — a genuine, if incremental, de-risking event ahead of the bigger March 2027 NCD decision.

What changed

Finalisation (not just proposal) of the FY2027 IPPS rule reassigning WiSE procedures to higher-paying DRG categories.

Financial impact

Higher per-procedure hospital reimbursement should support faster WiSE adoption from October 2026, but does not itself generate revenue for EBR. Not verified against any EBR revenue guidance.

Expectation / surprise

A largely expected, incremental positive — part of a multi-step CMS reimbursement pathway EBR has been building toward over the past year.

Key risks
  • The bigger catalyst — CMS's National Coverage Determination final decision — is not due until ~March 2027; today's news is a smaller, interim step.
  • EBR remains commercial-stage/early-revenue; reimbursement improvements take time to translate into sales.
What to watch into/after the close

EBR share-price confirmation; any broker commentary quantifying the earnings/revenue impact of the DRG reassignment.

Why it made the list: A fresh, material, negative guidance revision that is genuinely new to this report series and cuts directly against the day's otherwise-positive gold-sector framing.

What the market may be reacting to: Confirmation that Mali country/logistics risk is translating into a materially lower FY26 production and higher-cost outcome than previously guided.

What would confirm it: Continued underperformance versus gold-sector peers (e.g., RMS) into the close and over coming sessions; further downgrades if the Q4 fixes slip.

What would invalidate it: A rapid resolution of the Mali logistics/security issues and confirmed Q4 recovery to the guided 45–50koz.

Monitor: Contractor mobilisation and emulsion-plant commissioning progress reports; any further guidance revisions.

Why it made the list: A genuinely new exploration data point today with a clear, dated catalyst (October assays) to monitor — one of the few "clean" small-cap exploration stories surfaced this run.

What the market may be reacting to: First-ever drilling success (visually) at a new target (Bornite Hills) within an already-known, high-grade copper system (Rae Project).

What would confirm it: October assay results confirming high-grade copper (ideally consistent with or exceeding the historic 46.99% Cu trench result) across a meaningful width.

What would invalidate it: Assays returning low-grade or narrow intercepts despite visually promising logging.

Biggest risk: Binary, assay-dependent outcome in a highly speculative, remote-location micro-cap; extreme volatility likely in October regardless of outcome.

Monitor: Confirmation of the assay timeline; any interim drilling-program updates; broader Rae Project newsflow (Danvers, Reedy South).
