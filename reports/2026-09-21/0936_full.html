1.PPT — Perpetual Limited
Score 58/100 🟡 Mixed Takeover/M&A Price-sensitive: Yes

Announcement time: Not verified — secondary sources place an update "on 21 September" after a proposal reportedly lodged "Sunday evening" (20 September).

Announcement: EQT (via Windflower Pte) has returned with a further revised proposal for Perpetual at A$22.50/share (unchanged headline price from 27 July, reportedly with revised terms around an interim dividend of up to 60cps). Perpetual's board again declined to recommend it, citing "unacceptable" transaction-execution-risk assumptions, but offered EQT limited, non-exclusive due diligence access.

Why it matters

This is the largest live public M&A process on the ASX right now. A PE buyer returning a fourth time at an unchanged price while the target opens the data room suggests both sides see a deal as achievable but are negotiating risk allocation, not price. The eventual outcome is a binary, large-magnitude event for holders.

What changed

Perpetual moved from "hard rejection, no engagement" (1 July, 27 July) to "rejection but limited DD access" — a genuine, if modest, escalation.

Financial impact

Full acquisition at $22.50/share would represent a substantial premium to PPT's last known close (~$19.15, 18 Sept). Not verified as an exact percentage without a confirmed current price.

Expectation / surprise

Market had been pricing meaningful deal risk (PPT trading well below the proposal price for months); today's development is a modest positive surprise relative to a full breakdown, but not relative to consensus that further engagement was likely.

Market/sector context

Financials broadly are under pressure this month on hawkish RBA repricing (see Section 6); a take-private bid is somewhat insulated since valuation is buyer-driven.

Technical context

PPT closed ~A$19.15 on 18 Sept (+2.24% that session) — a persistent, large discount to the $22.50 proposal, consistent with the market pricing real deal-completion risk. Not independently chart-verified.

Key risks
  • Multiple sources give conflicting/overlapping prices and dates across this saga — the exact sequence could not be fully disambiguated without primary-source access.
  • EQT has walked away from Australian take-privates before when boards held firm.
  • Interim-dividend mechanics could dilute effective deal value relative to the headline $22.50.
What to watch

Any formal ASX announcement from Perpetual confirming/clarifying terms and DD access; PPT price action relative to $22.50 and $19.15.

Section 8 — Best 3 to Research Further

1. PPT — Perpetual

Why it made the list: The single largest, most clearly price-sensitive live situation identified this run, with a dated development from this weekend.

What the market may be reacting to: Whether EQT's willingness to keep returning at $22.50 (now with DD access) signals rising odds of a binding deal, versus stalling on risk allocation rather than price.

What would confirm it: A binding Scheme Implementation Deed, or a materially improved/de-risked proposal after due diligence.

What would invalidate it: A formal statement that EQT has ended engagement.

Biggest risk: Further rounds without resolution could see the stock drift back toward its standalone valuation, well below $22.50.

Monitor: Perpetual's ASX announcements page directly at market open; any EQT/Windflower statement.

2. FG1 — Flynn Gold

Why it made the list: A five-report-cycle-old, still-unresolved trading halt tied to a real capital raise, in a currently strong gold-price environment.

What the market may be reacting to: A $5m raise to fund Tasmanian exploration at a time when junior-gold economics are unusually favourable.

What would confirm it: Resumption at or above the raise price with strong take-up.

What would invalidate it: A steep-discount raise, weak demand, or continued suspension well past the expected window.

Biggest risk: Extreme dilution relative to a very small market cap; thin liquidity magnifies any move.

Monitor: ASX platform for FG1 resumption notice and Appendix 3B/2A/3G filings at market open.

3. Gold miners basket — CMM, NST, GMD

Why it made the list: A genuine, sustained sector tailwind at a moment when the broader ASX 200 is under macro pressure — one of the few areas with a positive, evidenced setup right now.

What the market may be reacting to: Continued safe-haven/rate-hedge demand for gold amid a hawkish RBA/global-rates backdrop.

What would confirm it: Continued gold-price strength into the open and/or production updates beating guidance.

What would invalidate it: A sharp reversal in the gold price (USD strength, risk-on rotation).

Biggest risk: A well-known, possibly crowded trade after a strong multi-week run.

Monitor: Spot gold intraday; Materials-sector relative performance vs. the ASX 200 at the open.
